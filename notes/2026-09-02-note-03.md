## 3. Policies at matched Ê₀[T] — first regret result

C̄ = 0.4, s = 0.5, change at ν = 0, 64 runs each. Oracle = better of its two operating points with τ_c known.

| τ_c/c | oracle | extremum-only | mid-fringe (bank) | interleave B=1 | interleave B=10 | explore 300 → ext | explore 1000 → ext |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 1 332 ± 114 | 2 084 (1.56×) | 1 299 (0.98×) | 1 934 (1.45×) | 2 044 (1.53×) | 1 546 (1.16×) | 1 147 (0.86×)† |
| 5 | 2 123 ± 184 | 2 123 (1.00×) | 2 920 (1.38×) | 2 830 (1.33×) | 2 680 (1.26×) | 2 003 (0.94×) | 2 220 (1.05×) |
| 1 | 2 217 ± 163 | 2 217 (1.00×) | 16 800 (7.6×) | 3 694 (1.67×) | 4 581 (2.07×) | 3 644 (1.64×) | 5 757 (2.6×)† |

† these two entries sit at ARL 26 300, ~12 % below target; treat as ~10 % optimistic.

Findings.

(a) Model-bank cost is nil. The learner that does not know τ_c but stays at mid-fringe matches the τ_c-knowing oracle at τ_c/c = 20 (0.98×). Lai-type second-order penalties for the unknown parameter are invisible at this γ. This settles card go/no-go item 5 in the affirmative for the *parameter-learning* part.

(b) The worst-case-optimal policy among those tested is the fixed extremum (max regret ratio 1.56×, at long τ_c). Every hedge — interleaving, or exploring at mid-fringe then switching on segment age — has worst case 1.64–1.67× and improves only the average. The mid-fringe upside (1.56× at τ_c/c = 20) is almost exactly cancelled by the cost of hedging against short τ_c. Blocked interleaving (B = 10) is worse than shot-by-shot interleaving (B = 1) at both extremes, because the extremum block spends its shots on a slower channel while the mid-fringe block loses correlation structure to the gap.

(c) Near the crossover (τ_c/c = 5) the mixed schedules are at or slightly below both pure channels (2 003 ± 140 vs 2 123) — consistent with the two channels' information adding when neither dominates. Within errors, but worth a targeted run.

(d) Interpretation for the card. The adaptive-regret question has a clear pilot answer at this operating point: learning τ_c is free, but there is nothing to gain from it in the worst case, because the regime map (note 01, panel ii) already places the extremum ahead almost everywhere once τ is optimised, and where mid-fringe wins it wins by ≤ 1.6×. The honest framing of contribution 7–8 is therefore "the regime map decides; adaptive scheduling recovers the mid-fringe upside only when a prior on τ_c justifies committing". This is not the "adaptive gain disappears" kill criterion (the learner does approach the oracle), but it does mean the paper's weight sits on the crossover, identifiability, and the map, not on the policy.

## 4. τ-scan identifiability corollary (card item 3)

Setting: T₂ = 10 t_dead, C̄₀ ∈ {0.4, 0.9}, gσ_xT₂ = 0.5 rad, uniform allocation over τ ∈ [0.1, 2] T₂, Markovian calibrated baseline χ̂ = τ/T₂ with relative calibration uncertainty η₀. Two admissible classes: (A) pointwise band |χ − χ̂| ≤ η₀χ̂; (B) Markov-parametric, χ = Γτ with |Γ − Γ̂| ≤ η₀Γ̂. The two-nuisance divergence 𝓘_id of card v1.1 is computed for each; class A decouples per τ (signal side takes the widest contrast, null side the narrowest), class B is a two-parameter infimum.

Per-shot 𝓘_id relative to the unconstrained divergence (same χ on both sides):

| C̄₀ | η₀ | τ_c/t_dead | unconstrained | class A | class B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.4 | 0.02 | 0.5 | 1.1e-6 | 0 | 2.5e-9 |
| 0.4 | 0.02 | 2 | 1.2e-5 | 4.4e-8 | 2.7e-7 |
| 0.4 | 0.02 | 10 | 6.9e-5 | 2.7e-5 | 2.7e-5 |
| 0.4 | 0.02 | 50 | 1.2e-4 | 6.4e-5 | 6.4e-5 |
| 0.4 | 0.05 | 10 | 6.9e-5 | 1.8e-6 | 6.6e-6 |
| 0.4 | 0.10 | 50 | 1.2e-4 | 7.5e-8 | 1.6e-5 |
| 0.9 | 0.02 | 10 | 3.7e-4 | 1.4e-4 | 1.4e-4 |
| 0.9 | 0.05 | 50 | 6.5e-4 | 8.9e-5 | 1.2e-4 |

(full table in `sid_run7.py` output). Three statements follow.

(i) For τ_c ≲ T₂/5 the extremum signal is unidentifiable against a dephasing-rate change under either class: class B retains 10⁻³–10⁻² of the unconstrained information, class A nothing. The closed-form criterion in the τ ≫ τ_c limit is ΔΓ_φ = (gσ_x)²τ_c > 2η₀Γ̂: the induced dephasing rate must exceed the calibration uncertainty of the baseline rate. This is exactly the "extremum is a T₂ measurement" statement made quantitative.

(ii) For τ_c ≳ T₂ the Gaussian-in-τ signature is identifiable: class B keeps 20–50 % of the unconstrained information at η₀ = 2 %, 10–15 % at 5–10 %. Identification costs a factor 2–10 in shots relative to detection.

(iii) A pointwise band is too weak a class to carry the shape argument (it lets the null mimic any curve within the band); the identifiability claim in the card requires a shape-constrained baseline class, and the paper should state class B (or a smoothness-constrained generalisation) as the admissible class, with η₀ tied to the calibration protocol.

Since the regime where the extremum wins the detection race (short τ_c) is the regime where it cannot identify, the card's sentence "the extremum branch detects a change in dephasing; it identifies stochastic frequency noise only when…" is now backed by numbers, and the "only when" is τ_c ≳ T₂ with a shape-constrained baseline.

## 5. Servo regime — four-outcome likelihood, first step (card item 2)

Oscillator phase von Mises with concentration κ, mean tracked (μ = 0), independent between shots; C̄₁ = C̄₂ = 0.9; phases (φ₁, φ₂) optimised on a 13 × 13 grid. Two channels mapped to an equivalent binary-link contrast: slope channel from the Fisher information about ε at ε = 0 (C_eff = √FI), variance channel from the exact KL of a white s = 0.3 modulation solved for the C̄ that gives the same D_Bern.

| κ | slope, parity only | slope, four-outcome | variance, parity only | variance, four-outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.405 | 0.405 | 0.405 | 0.405 |
| 1 | 0.448 | 0.510 | 0.448 | 0.538 |
| 2 | 0.527 | 0.655 | 0.527 | 0.675 |
| 5 | 0.665 | 0.813 | 0.665 | 0.807 |
| 10 | 0.733 | 0.856 | 0.733 | 0.854 |
| ∞ | 0.810 | 0.900 | 0.810 | 0.900 |

Findings. The two channels have the same effective contrast to within 2 % at every κ, so the binary-link theorem carries over to the partial-coherence regime with C̄ → C_eff(κ) and the crossover table unchanged in form. The individual bits add up to 0.13 in C_eff over parity alone at intermediate κ. Two corrections to card wording: the parity cap ½C̄₁C̄₂ applies to the *fully randomised* oscillator only — at κ → ∞ the parity contrast is C̄₁C̄₂ (0.81 here), because no phase averaging remains; and C_eff > 0.8 requires κ ≳ 5, i.e. the servo must hold the oscillator phase to ≲ 0.45 rad rms over an interrogation. The correlated common-mode residual (shot-to-shot dependence of the oscillator phase, which mimics the signal's correlation channel) is not in this calculation and remains the open item for the servo branch.
