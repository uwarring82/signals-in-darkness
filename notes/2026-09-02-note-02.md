# Signals in Darkness — execution note 02 (2 Sept 2026): pre-v1.1 checks

Three checks requested before issuing v1.1. Scripts: `sid_run4.py` (checks 2 and 3a, first calibration attempt), `sid_run5.py` (check 3b, corrected calibration). All at C̄ = 0.4, s = 0.5 unless stated.

## Check 2 — geometric-fit closed form vs exact-r_k spectral integral

Exact lag correlation r_k = C̄²e^{−s²} sinh(s²A a^k) with A = F(τ/τ_c) the filter factor and a = e^{−c/τ_c}. Expanding sinh in its Taylor series makes the spectrum an exact finite sum of geometric spectra,

S(ω) − 1 = Σ_m 2ρ_m (a_m cos ω − a_m²)/(1 − 2a_m cos ω + a_m²),  ρ_m = C̄²e^{−s²}(s²A)^{2m+1}/(2m+1)!,  a_m = a^{2m+1},

six terms sufficing for s²A ≤ 1. The rate ½⟨S − 1 − log S⟩_ω is then a 1024-point quadrature per evaluation — as cheap as the geometric closed form and exact in r_k. Verified against the direct 6000-lag sum to all printed digits in three cases.

Over the full τ-optimised map domain (C̄₀ ∈ [0.05, 0.995], τ_c/t_dead ∈ [0.1, 10³], T₂ = 10 t_dead, gσ_xT₂ = 0.5 rad), evaluated at the mid-fringe-optimal τ: geometric-fit/series − 1 has maximum |0.38 %|, median 0.01 %; the winner boundary differs on 0.03 % of grid points. The only place the shortcut fails is large s: point-sampled crossover at s = 1.0 is 34 vs 40 (C̄ = 0.4), 25 vs 29 (0.7), 249 vs 323 (0.9); at s ≤ 0.5 the two agree to 2 %. (The "max over all τ" statistic in the run output is dominated by ratios of numerically zero rates at C̄₀ = 0.05, τ_c/t_dead = 0.5 and is not meaningful.)

Decision for v1.1: the series form is the working formula for the principal figure. The geometric closed form −½ log[α/(1 + b²)] is retained as its leading (m = 0) term, valid for s²A ≲ 0.3. Neither is called the exact binary rate.

## Check 3a — grid convergence of the latent-AR(1) reference

Four cases (mid-fringe at (0.4, 0.5, 20), (0.9, 0.3, 5), (0.4, 1.0, 20); extremum at (0.9, 0.3, 5)), same seed across settings so that only discretisation varies. Grid sizes M = 41 … 241 and half-widths L = 4 … 7 s change the rate estimate by < 0.01 % (one case −0.03 % at L = 4). The reference is grid-converged at M ≥ 41, L ≥ 4.

The limiting error is Monte-Carlo: at N = 10⁵ the per-shot rate estimate has ≈ 5–10 % standard error (e.g. 3.42e-3 with seed 5 vs 3.09e-3 with seed 3 at (0.4, 0.5, 20); series comparator 3.05e-3). Pinning a single point to 1 % needs N ≈ 10⁷ or ~100 seeds. The earlier "GP comparator agrees with HMM to 1–5 %" is therefore "agrees within Monte-Carlo error"; the series form is the more precise number in every case. Description for v1.1: "grid-converged latent-AR(1) binary reference; Monte-Carlo standard error ≈ 7 % at N = 10⁵".

One point sharpened: at the extremum with C̄ = 0.9, s = 0.3 the reference gives 4.16e-3 (seed 5) and 3.90(18)e-3 (three seeds earlier) against the marginal D_Bern 3.69e-3. A ~6–12 % excess from bit correlations in the extremum stream is now seen on four seeds; it is probably real but needs a long run to state. If confirmed, the extremum formula is a lower bound at high contrast.

## Check 3b — false-alarm calibration and delays at matched E₀[T]

First attempt (`sid_run4.py`) extrapolated log ARL linearly from h ∈ [4, 6] down to the h giving ARL = 10³ and landed the two detectors at ARL 51 and 265: invalid, discarded.

Corrected (`sid_run5.py`): bisection on h until measured E₀[T] = 3 × 10⁴ (target chosen so that h sits inside the log-linear range), 120–240 null runs for the extremum detector, 24–48 for the mid-fringe filter-CUSUM.

| detector | h* | measured ARL | e^{h*} | ARL / e^{h*} |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| extremum | 3.70 | 30 300 ± 1 900 | 40 | 750 |
| mid-fringe, τ_c/c = 20 | 4.15 | 37 400 ± 5 600 | 64 | 590 |
| mid-fringe, τ_c/c = 5 | 3.17 | 26 100 ± 3 000 | 24 | 1 100 |
| mid-fringe, τ_c/c = 1 | 1.69 | 39 100 ± 4 500 | 5 | 7 200 |

The predictable-action likelihood-ratio bound E₀T ≥ e^h holds but is loose by 10²–10⁴ here, as expected for small increments (Siegmund-type ARL₀ ≈ (e^h − h − 1)/D₀ with D₀ ≈ 10⁻³). It cannot be used for calibration; my scouting remark that policy-specific false-alarm control is "easier than the card fears" was wrong in practice. Simulation, or a corrected approximation, is required for every action.

Delays at matched E₀[T] ≈ 3 × 10⁴, change at ν = 0:

| τ_c/c | extremum | mid-fringe | delay ratio ext/mid | rate ratio I_mid/I_ext |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 2 140 ± 110 | 1 370 ± 140 | 1.56 | 2.36 |
| 5 | 2 170 ± 100 | 2 660 ± 280 | 0.82 | 0.69 |
| 1 | 2 160 ± 110 | 12 100 ± 1 400 | 0.18 | 0.06 |

Two results. The delay crossover lies between τ_c/c = 5 and 20 and is consistent with the series-comparator crossover of 7.2 at this (C̄, s). And the delay advantage is compressed relative to the rate advantage — 1.56 against 2.36 on the mid-fringe side, 5.6 against 17 on the extremum side — because at finite γ the winning detector needs a higher threshold for the same null run length and the losing one benefits from reflection. The card's "finite false-alarm regime" is exactly where asymptotic rate ratios overstate the case by roughly a factor 1.5–3; the crossover location itself is robust.
