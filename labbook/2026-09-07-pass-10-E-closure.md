# 2026-09-07 — Pass 10: agreement is not correctness

**Question:** Is E's class-B infimum actually converged, and was C13 correctly handled by rewriting it in place?
**Finding:** No to both. The `start_spread` note 15 leaned on was an **absolute** objective difference, and I_B spans four orders of magnitude, so at τ_c/T₂ = 0.05 — where I_B is of order 10⁻⁹ — a spread of 2.4 × 10⁻⁹ is a 12 % disagreement. Measured relatively, 5 of 36 rows disagree by more than 0.1 % and 4 by more than 1 %, the worst at 12.07 % with solutions 0.091 apart. The test guarding the claim asserted the absolute figure, so it passed while the claim was false. Validating the discordant rows against a deterministic grid-and-polish then produced the sharper result: on five rows the grid beat **every** named start, by up to 9.7 %, and those were the rows where the named starts agreed *most* closely — 0.003 % to 0.115 %. On the worst, all three starts sat 9.7 % above a minimum lying on the g₀ boundary. Across-start agreement was anti-correlated with correctness.
**Why it matters:** All five are principal-regime rows at short correlation time, which is exactly where the identifiability claim's headline sits, so a best-of-three would have published wrong numbers on the clause that matters most. The grid is now part of the method rather than a diagnostic — it supplies the selected minimum on 11 of 36 rows, and being seed-free it keeps E deterministic. The reported I_B is stated as a best-of-candidates estimate, not a certified global infimum, and a test asserts the artifact does not claim otherwise. The regression gate was also too generous: it treated any sufficiently lower I_B as an under-converged reference, which would let a real regression pass whenever it pointed downward. It now requires both an explicit allowlist entry and unanimity across all candidates, with everything else landing in `review_required` and failing `--check`.
