# 2026-09-08 — Pass 17: a difference of 0.03 on an error bar of 0.25

**Question:** With the pilot properly calibrated, does the hedge ordering invert between operating points?
**Finding:** Not as stated, and the corrected version is stronger. Recalibrated, the pilot's point estimates put interleave-B1 first at 1.60 against fixed-extremum's 1.63 — which read as an ordering would have overturned C11. Propagating the delay standard errors of numerator and benchmark, that difference is 0.03 ± 0.25, **0.1 σ**. It does not resolve, and neither does switch@300. What does resolve at the pilot is that switch@1000 is 2.7 σ *worse* and learner-mid 5.0 σ worse. So "four of five hedges beat fixed-extremum" was a point-estimate count; two of five survive propagation.
**Why it matters:** The claim that survives is sharper than the one it replaces. **switch@1000 reverses with both directions resolved** — 2.7 σ worse at the pilot, 3.1 σ better at the stress point, both operating points inside the ±30 % envelope. That is a reversal of a resolved ordering rather than of point estimates, and it is about one named schedule rather than hedging in general. C11 survives untouched in substance, and its own closing clause — that ARL precision does not order them — turns out to have been the correct reading all along.
