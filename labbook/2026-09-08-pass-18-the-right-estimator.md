# 2026-09-08 — Pass 18: pairing and selection pull opposite ways

**Question:** Are the worst-case delay-ratio orderings actually resolved, given that policies share seeds and the statistic is a maximum over three correlation times?
**Finding:** Mostly yes, and the count changes again. A paired bootstrap over per-replicate stopping times — resampling jointly within each source so the shared-seed pairing survives, independently between sources because `res8` uses a different seed schedule from the `res6` benchmark it divides by, and recomputing the maximum inside every draw so selection bias is carried — confirms the central result and revises one count. At the pilot, no hedge is resolvedly better than fixed-extremum: `interleave-B1` is +0.03 [−0.36, +0.39], P(better) = 0.550. At the stress point, **three** of five are resolved, not the two that marginal propagation gave and not the four that point estimates gave: `interleave-B10` sits at 1.9 σ marginally and resolves at [+0.22, +1.55] once pairing is respected. `switch@1000` reverses with both directions resolved, P = 0.002 worse at the pilot and P = 1.000 better at the stress point.
**Why it matters:** The two effects pull opposite ways — pairing makes a difference look more significant, selection makes it look less — so neither could be waved away by arguing the other dominates. Getting it right needed per-replicate stopping times, which the drivers were discarding; they now store them.
