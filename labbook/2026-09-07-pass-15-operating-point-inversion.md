# 2026-09-07 — Pass 15: the answer depends on where you stand

**Question:** Does the demotion of adaptivity survive a second operating point?
**Finding:** No. It reverses. At the parity-ceiling stress point (C_eff = 0.5, s = 0.3) fixed-extremum's worst-case delay ratio degrades to 2.88 while four of five hedges beat it, the best — `switch@1000` — at 1.51. At the pilot the same table has fixed-extremum best at 1.44 and no hedge beating it, and `switch@1000` is the *worst* hedge at 2.60. The cleanest anchor is those two policies at the stress point: both miss the ARL target by exactly +13 %, so the 1.91× gap between them cannot be blamed on calibration mismatch. Zero capped delay runs at either operating point.
**Why it matters:** The card's stated reason for running B was that "the demotion of adaptivity must rest on two points, not one." It does not rest on two. The comparison was only possible because the pilot chain was re-run from scratch on its own fresh thresholds first: every published pilot ratio divides by a τ_c/c = 20 benchmark withdrawn as C18, so the archived numbers could not be set beside the stress ones. That re-run reproduced the archive at 15/15 delay rows and 6/6 switch rows while still failing on exactly C17's two withdrawn calibrations — which both validates the fresh chain and removes the withdrawn dependency that had held C09, C11 and C12 `open`. All three move to `result` with corrected numbers, since replacing a withdrawn benchmark changes every ratio built on it.
**Status:** C09, C11, C12 → `result` (repaired basis, scopes narrowed). C27 added, `result`: the inversion. Ledger now 27 claims, 18 result, 1 pilot, 1 open, 7 withdrawn.
**Next test:** roadmap C — posterior-driven action selection, the one thing this result explicitly does not speak to.

Technical record: notes/2026-09-07-note-18-roadmap-B.md. The framing that must survive into the manuscript is that **every schedule tested is precommitted**: `sched(n)` takes the step index and cannot consult the posterior, and `run_batch` calls it that way, so although the banks mix over τ_c and the filter does learn, the action rule never uses what it learns — `switch@B` switches on segment age, not on the posterior. C27 is therefore about robust action *scheduling* across unknown correlation times, not about online learning succeeding, and a reader who takes the latter from it would be taking something the run cannot support. Two deviations are recorded rather than smoothed: `learner-interleave-B1` calibrates to +31.8 % at the stress point, outside the ±30 % envelope, which biases its delay upward and so runs against the hedge rather than for it; and C12's "no resolved penalty" turns out to hold only at τ_c/c = 20 and 5, with the bank costing 1.60× at τ_c/c = 1 where a mixture over {1, 4, 10, 25} is furthest from the truth. The audit that preceded the run earned its cost: under the pilot's bracket, `oracle-mid(tc=1)` at the stress point would have returned a threshold pinned to an untested bracket end, missing the matched-ARL target by 21 % while reporting a clean calibration.
