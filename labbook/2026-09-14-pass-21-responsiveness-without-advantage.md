# 2026-09-14 — Pass 21: the policy responds, and is slower anyway

**Question:** Does posterior-driven action selection detect a change sooner than a precommitted schedule?
**Finding:** Mostly no, and the negative is cleaner than a win would have been. Against **L1**, the best fixed endpoint selected independently per correlation time, posterior sampling is resolvedly **slower** in five of six cells, 1.19 to 2.14; the sixth is unresolved. Against **L2**, the best eligible robust precommitted schedule, it is faster twice, slower once and unresolved three times. The independently selected **L3** procedure, ranging over all thirteen eligible candidates, picks a constant endpoint in five cells and sampling in one. Meanwhile the policy genuinely does respond: its action frequency shifts with the true correlation time by resolved margins at both operating points, while the greedy variant adapts its beliefs and barely changes what it does.
**Why it matters:** Belief is not behaviour, and behaviour is not performance. The preregistration had recorded in advance that neither balance nor responsiveness implies a shorter delay; that warning is now the finding. The physics shows through the benchmark: L1 picks mid-fringe at long correlation times and the extremum at short ones — the crossover this project is about — and a policy that must infer the correlation time pays for the inference inside the detection horizon.
**Status:** Selection frozen from the `700 + tau_c` stream before any evaluation was read; evaluation run on the disjoint `200 + tau_c` stream. All twelve learner evaluation cells reproduce `sid_run10` **per replicate**, so the common driver is verified against the outputs it replaces. Hard falsifier passed: no learner beats the diagnostic envelope it is minimised inside.
**Next test:** whether the ±30 % calibration band, rather than the policy, produced the gap.

Technical record: notes/2026-09-14-note-23-ladder-result-and-corrections.md. Two limits belong with the result rather than after it. L3 selected **sampling** at stress tau_c = 5, which is legal because both learners are in its class by design, but that cell is then the learner re-measured on held-out data and cannot evidence that the learner beats a benchmark. And several selection margins are a fraction of a standard error — pilot tau_c = 20 separates `mid` from `switch@3000` by 0.02 se — so a different selection draw would routinely name a different member. The frozen choice is still scored honestly on held-out data; the caveat bounds what the choice means, not whether it is valid.
