# 2026-09-05 — Pass 5: where the operating-point result actually begins

**Question:** Is the crossover between the two operating points already in the literature — and if part of it is, which part is this project's?
**Finding:** The *mechanism* is prior art and must be conceded. Wudarski, Zhang, Korotkov, Petukhov and Dykman (Phys. Rev. Applied 19, 064066) work in this project's exact setting — periodically repeated Ramsey shots, one binary outcome each, exponentially correlated frequency noise — and give the single-outcome signal ∝ cos φ_R alongside a pair correlator ∝ f_k sin²φ_R, then propose "measuring the pair and triple correlators for different values of φ_R and comparing the results". Expanding their exact correlator gives f_k/4 at mid-fringe against f_k²/8 at the extremum: first order against second, so the relative merit of the two operating points *cannot* be amplitude-independent. That is the mechanism behind the crossover, published. What was not found within the searched boundary is the quantitative object: a crossover locus in τ_c/c, its motion with s (4.65 → 5.46 → 7.24 at C̄ = 0.4), its absence at C̄ = 0.99, or any treatment of detection delay at matched false-alarm rate for a latent AR(1) binary record.
**Why it matters:** C05 is re-scoped rather than withdrawn — the claim is the detection-theoretic boundary and its amplitude dependence, not the discovery that the two operating points differ. The overlap the card had flagged turned out to be a false friend: Sakuldee and Cywiński's "optimal working point" is the quadratic-coupling sweet spot, not a Ramsey readout phase, so both papers remain must-cite for the measurement-stream formalism rather than for operating-point competition.
**Status:** result (C05, re-scoped); unchanged (C08, C13, C23).
