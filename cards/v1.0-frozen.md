# Signals in Darkness: Operating-Point Crossover in Sequential Quantum-Clock Detection

**Status:** Frozen v1.0  
**Endpoint:** Compact theory paper with one principal theorem, one adaptive-regret result, and one two-dimensional regime map  
**Application:** Abstract paired-clock comparison; application-specific models deferred

## Research question

How should synchronously interrogated quantum clocks choose their Ramsey operating point and interrogation timing to detect weak stochastic frequency modulation as quickly as possible?

The paper addresses:

1. When does fringe-extremum operation, which detects contrast loss, outperform mid-fringe operation, which detects temporal correlations?
2. When can the extremum signal be distinguished from an ordinary change in \(T_2\)?
3. What wall-clock delay is incurred when the signal correlation time is unknown and must be learned while measurements are selected?

The central contribution is the interaction between identifiability, model learning, and measurement selection.

## Motivation

A zero-mean stochastic phase modulation appears differently at different positions on a Ramsey fringe.

- At an extremum, it changes the marginal bit probability through contrast loss.
- At mid-fringe, it leaves the mean unchanged but creates temporal correlations.

The preferred measurement therefore depends on the hidden process’s correlation time. Moreover, the extremum response can be indistinguishable from ordinary dephasing unless the experiment varies its interrogation duration.

This problem arises in differential clock comparisons, stochastic-field sensing, and qubit noise spectroscopy. The paper studies the general detection boundary rather than a particular clock platform or application.

## Paired-clock observation model

Two clocks or qubits are synchronously interrogated by a common local oscillator. Their outcomes are

\[
B_{1,n},B_{2,n}\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

Conditional on oscillator phase \(\varphi_n\), differential signal phase \(\epsilon_n\), and action \(u_n\),

\[
P(B_{1,n},B_{2,n}\mid\varphi_n,\epsilon_n,u_n)
=
\frac14
\left[
1+B_{1,n}\bar C_1(\tau_n)
\cos(\varphi_n+\phi_{1,n})
\right]
\left[
1+B_{2,n}\bar C_2(\tau_n)
\cos(\varphi_n+\epsilon_n+\phi_{2,n})
\right].
\]

The effective contrasts include decoherence and readout loss:

\[
\bar C_j(\tau)
=
\bar C_{0,j}
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{\tau}{2T_{1,j}}-\chi_{\phi,j}(\tau)
\right].
\]

Under exponential Markovian decoherence,

\[
\bar C_j(\tau)=\bar C_{0,j}e^{-\tau/T_{2,j}}.
\]

Distinct \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) values therefore enter through the achievable contrast and interrogation window, without requiring separate models for different hardware platforms.

The Ramsey filter function is denoted \(m_n(t)\). Common-mode oscillator rejection requires

\[
\tau_{1,n}=\tau_{2,n},
\qquad
m_{1,n}(t)=m_{2,n}(t).
\]

If the filters differ, residual oscillator noise remains explicitly in the likelihood.

## Two oscillator regimes

### Fully randomized oscillator: parity readout

Marginalizing a uniformly random oscillator phase gives

\[
P(B_{1,n},B_{2,n}\mid\epsilon_n)
=
\frac14
\left[
1+
\frac12B_{1,n}B_{2,n}
\bar C_1\bar C_2
\cos(\epsilon_n+\Delta\phi_n)
\right].
\]

The informative statistic is the parity

\[
Z_n=B_{1,n}B_{2,n},
\]

with effective contrast

\[
\boxed{
\bar C_{\mathrm{par}}
=
\frac12\bar C_1\bar C_2
\leq\frac12
}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
p_0=\frac{1-\bar C_{\mathrm{par}}}{2}\geq\frac14.
\]

Parity correlation spectroscopy never produces a genuinely dark fringe. For realistic individual contrasts \(\bar C_j\simeq0.9\),

\[
\bar C_{\mathrm{par}}\simeq0.405.
\]

### Partially coherent oscillator: full joint readout

For a partially coherent oscillator, use

\[
q_\kappa(\varphi)
=
\frac{
e^{\kappa\cos(\varphi-\mu)}
}{
2\pi I_0(\kappa)
}.
\]

The mean phase \(\mu\) must be tracked by a servo. Servo interrogations and their dead time are part of the wall-clock cost for both the oracle and adaptive learner.

The physical likelihood is

\[
P(B_1,B_2\mid\epsilon,u)
=
\int
P(B_1,B_2\mid\varphi,\epsilon,u)
q_\kappa(\varphi)\,d\varphi .
\]

When \(\kappa>0\), the individual bits carry information in addition to parity. Effective contrasts above \(1/2\), including near-dark fringes, belong to this regime and require the full four-outcome likelihood with residual oscillator noise.

The principal figure will distinguish:

- \(\bar C\leq1/2\): parity-only, oscillator-cancelled regime;
- \(\bar C>1/2\): servo-tracked, partially coherent regime.

## Hidden process

The first paper uses stationary Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck frequency noise:

\[
\mathbb E[x(t)]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[x(t)x(t+\Delta)]
=
\sigma_x^2e^{-|\Delta|/\tau_c}.
\]

The accumulated Ramsey phase is

\[
\epsilon_n
=
g\int_{t_n}^{t_n+\tau}
m_n(t)x(t)\,dt.
\]

For free Ramsey evolution,

\[
s_\tau^2
=
\operatorname{Var}(\epsilon_n)
=
2g^2\sigma_x^2
\left[
\tau_c\tau
-\tau_c^2
\left(
1-e^{-\tau/\tau_c}
\right)
\right].
\]

Therefore,

\[
s_\tau^2\sim
\begin{cases}
g^2\sigma_x^2\tau^2,
& \tau\ll\tau_c,\\[4pt]
2g^2\sigma_x^2\tau_c\tau,
& \tau\gg\tau_c.
\end{cases}
\]

The normalized phase correlation is

\[
a_k(\tau,c)
=
\frac{
\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon_n,\epsilon_{n+k})
}{
s_\tau^2
}.
\]

The approximation \(a_k\simeq e^{-kc/\tau_c}\) is used only in the point-sampling limit.

The known signal quantity is \(g\sigma_x\). The learner does not know \(\tau_c\), and therefore does not know \(s_\tau\). At mid-fringe, the correlations

\[
r_k=\bar C^2s_\tau^2a_k
\]

contain \(\tau_c\) through both their amplitude and lag dependence. Two or more lags make the correlation scale learnable in principle.

## Null and the meaning of the extremum branch

The null includes a calibrated differential-decoherence baseline:

\[
H_0:
\quad
\epsilon_n=0,
\qquad
\bar C_j(\tau,n)
=
\bar C_{j,0}(\tau)+\delta C_{j,n}.
\]

The nuisance \(\delta C_{j,n}\) covers differential \(T_2\) variation, laser-power drift, readout drift, and unexplained dephasing.

At the extremum, the OU process produces

\[
\bar C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau)
=
\bar C_0(\tau)e^{-s_\tau^2/2}.
\]

For \(\tau\gg\tau_c\),

\[
e^{-s_\tau^2/2}
\simeq
e^{-g^2\sigma_x^2\tau_c\tau}.
\]

This is exactly exponential in \(\tau\) and is indistinguishable from an additional Markovian dephasing rate

\[
\Delta\Gamma_\phi=g^2\sigma_x^2\tau_c.
\]

Thus, in the short-correlation regime where the crossover favors the extremum, the extremum is fundamentally a \(T_2\) measurement. Fixed-\(\tau\) contrast calibration cannot identify the physical cause.

For \(\tau\ll\tau_c\),

\[
e^{-s_\tau^2/2}
\simeq
e^{-g^2\sigma_x^2\tau^2/2},
\]

which has a Gaussian-in-\(\tau\) signature distinct from Markovian \(e^{-\tau/T_2}\) decay.

Specific attribution to stochastic frequency noise therefore requires a scan over \(\tau\). The action set performs identifiability work, not merely rate optimization.

Echo interleaving does not generally solve the problem: it suppresses slowly varying noise most effectively when \(\tau_c\gg\tau\), precisely where the correlation channel is already favored.

A permissible final conclusion is therefore:

> The extremum branch detects a change in dephasing. It identifies stochastic frequency noise only when the accessible \(\tau\)-dependence is demonstrably nonexponential.

## Principal operating-point theorem

Consider the calibrated effective binary link

\[
P(Y_n=1\mid\epsilon_n,\theta)
=
\frac12
\left[
1+\bar C\cos(\theta+\epsilon_n)
\right].
\]

### Extremum information

At \(\theta=\pi\),

\[
p_0=\frac{1-\bar C}{2},
\qquad
p_1=
\frac{1-\bar C e^{-s^2/2}}{2}.
\]

The marginal information is exactly

\[
I_{\mathrm{ext}}
=
D_{\mathrm{Bern}}
\left(
\frac{1-\bar C e^{-s^2/2}}{2}
\;\middle\Vert\;
\frac{1-\bar C}{2}
\right).
\]

For small \(s\),

\[
I_{\mathrm{ext}}
=
\frac{\bar C^2s^4}
{8(1-\bar C^2)}
+o(s^4),
\]

provided

\[
s^2\ll
\frac{1-\bar C^2}{\bar C}.
\]

### Mid-fringe information

At \(\theta=\pi/2\), the marginal probability remains \(1/2\), while

\[
r_k
=
\bar C^2s^2a_k+O(s^4).
\]

The leading relative-entropy rate is

\[
I_{\mathrm{mid}}
=
\frac12\sum_{k\ge1}r_k^2+o(s^4)
=
\frac12\bar C^4s^4
\sum_{k\ge1}a_k^2
+o(s^4).
\]

This requires

\[
\bar C^4s^4
\sum_{k\ge1}a_k^2
\ll1.
\]

### Crossover

Where both expansions are valid, mid-fringe operation wins when

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\ge1}a_k^2
>
\frac{1}
{4\bar C^2(1-\bar C^2)}
}.
\]

| Effective contrast | Threshold | Physical interpretation |
|---:|---:|---|
| \(0.40\) | \(1.86\) | Realistic parity contrast for \(\bar C_j\simeq0.9\) |
| \(0.50\) | \(1.33\) | Ideal parity upper bound |
| \(0.70\) | \(1.00\) | Partially coherent oscillator |
| \(0.90\) | \(1.62\) | High-coherence servo regime |
| \(0.99\) | \(12.8\) | Near-dark coherent-oscillator limit |

## Endpoint-optimality lemma

With

\[
x=\cos^2\theta,
\]

the leading information has the form

\[
I(x)
=
A\frac{x}{1-\bar C^2x}
+
B\frac{(1-x)^2}{(1-\bar C^2x)^2},
\]

where

\[
A=\frac{\bar C^2s^4}{8},
\qquad
B=\frac12\bar C^4s^4\sum_{k\ge1}a_k^2.
\]

The mean and correlation perturbations are orthogonal in the null \(\chi^2\) metric: the former is a one-body centered statistic and the latter a two-body centered statistic. Their information contributions therefore add at leading order.

The derivative numerator is

\[
A(1-\bar C^2x)
-
2B(1-\bar C^2)(1-x).
\]

It is linear in \(x\) and positive at \(x=1\). Hence it is either positive throughout or crosses once from negative to positive, producing an interior minimum. The optimum is always an endpoint under balanced readout.

Asymmetric readout may move the optimum inward and will be checked using the exact likelihood.

## Adaptive policy

The clean information about \(\tau_c\) is contained in mid-fringe or intermediate-phase correlations. Extremum-only data are confounded with contrast and \(T_2\).

The adaptive policy therefore has a constrained structure:

1. maintain the common-oscillator servo;
2. begin at mid-fringe and measure at least two temporal lags;
3. update the posterior over \(\tau_c\);
4. remain at mid-fringe when the correlated regime is supported;
5. migrate toward the extremum only when short correlation is supported;
6. interleave multiple \(\tau\) values before attributing extremum contrast loss to stochastic frequency noise rather than a \(T_2\) change.

If the \(\tau\)-signature cannot distinguish the two, the output is explicitly “additional dephasing detected,” not “OU frequency noise identified.”

The oracle knows \(\tau_c\) and \(g\sigma_x\), but not the realized trajectory or change time. The learner knows \(g\sigma_x\) and the OU family, but not \(\tau_c\). Both pay the same servo and calibration costs.

For

\[
\mathbb E_0[T]\geq\gamma,
\]

the learning regret is

\[
R_{\mathrm{learn}}(\tau_c;\gamma)
=
\mathbb E_{\tau_c}[T_{\mathrm{adaptive}}-\nu]
-
\mathbb E_{\tau_c}[T_{\mathrm{oracle}}-\nu].
\]

False-alarm calibration is policy-specific because the null bit law changes with \(\theta\), \(\tau\), and servo state.

## Principal figure

The sole regime map uses

\[
\left(
\bar C,\frac{\tau_c}{c_{\min}}
\right)
\]

after optimizing the permitted \(\tau\).

It must show:

- the parity ceiling \(\bar C=1/2\);
- the realistic parity reference \(\bar C\simeq0.4\);
- the extremum/mid-fringe crossover;
- the optimized \(\tau/T_2\);
- the regimes \(\tau\ll\tau_c\) and \(\tau\gg\tau_c\);
- where the extremum is specific, dephasing-only, or unidentifiable;
- where the mid-fringe perturbative condition fails;
- the boundary where predicted delay is comparable to \(\tau_c\);
- parity-only versus servo-tracked physical realizations.

No asymptotic formula will be extrapolated through a marked invalidity region.

## Go/no-go conditions

Proceed only if:

1. the full paired-clock likelihood preserves the crossover;
2. the crossover intersects physically accessible parity or servo regimes;
3. the extremum advantage survives the contrast-nuisance model;
4. the accessible \(\tau\)-signature distinguishes the extremum signal from a dephasing-rate change;
5. the adaptive learner approaches the achievable oracle with interpretable wall-clock regret;
6. useful actions remain compatible with common-mode rejection and servo costs;
7. the result is not a direct substitution into an existing active-testing theorem.

Stop or reinterpret the extremum branch if it can establish only that \(T_2\) changed. Stop the paper entirely if the remaining result reduces to ordinary sequential discrimination of known Bernoulli channels.
