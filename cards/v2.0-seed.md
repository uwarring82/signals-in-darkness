## Committed paper hierarchy

1. **Detection-theoretic operating-point crossover and physical regime map.** The one-shot contrast-loss response at a fringe extremum and the pair-correlation response at mid-fringe are established Ramsey-noise-spectroscopy mechanisms (Wudarski et al. 2023). The project's claim is their sequential-information boundary: theorem in the s → 0 limit (amplitude-free); finite-amplitude working rate via the validated spectral comparator built from the exact lag correlations; two-panel figure (same-τ theorem illustration; τ-optimised physical map parameterised by gσ_xT₂ and T₂/t_dead). Status: result at one parameter set; second parameter set required (see gating runs).
2. **Detection-versus-identification boundary under a shape-constrained dephasing baseline.** Two-nuisance identifiability divergence 𝓘_id over an admissible class 𝒳₀; theorem-level statement that a pointwise uncertainty band yields no identifiability at short correlation time and that the τ-scan argument requires shape (Markov-parametric or smoothness-bounded class); closed-form fast-noise criterion ΔΓ_φ > 2η₀Γ̂; numerical retained-information table. Equal billing with item 1.
3. **Extension to the independently randomised servo model through C_eff(κ).** Stated as supported in the tested independent-phase, small-modulation model (both channels within 2 %); the correlated common-mode residual is excluded from the paper and named as the open extension. Decision recorded: the paper's principal claims live in the oscillator-cancelled parity regime (C̄ ≤ ½); C_eff(κ) is an appendix.
4. **Policy comparison.** Pilot appendix unless the posterior-driven run (below) upgrades it. Wording fixed by note 03a: best fixed-endpoint benchmark, worst-case delay ratio, composite-model detection penalty unresolved, conclusion limited to tested policies and τ_c ∈ {1, 5, 20}.
