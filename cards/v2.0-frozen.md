## Research question and model

How does the Ramsey operating point determine which evidence reveals stochastic frequency
noise, and when does detecting that evidence identify its physical cause?

The observation model remains the synchronous paired-clock model of v1.1. Its four-outcome
likelihood, matched interrogation filters, parity reduction and von Mises partial-coherence
model are retained from [v1.1's observation specification](v1.1-frozen.md#paired-clock-observation-model).
The principal claims concern the oscillator-cancelled parity regime with uniformly random
oscillator phases, independent from shot to shot and of the signal phase, and matched
interrogation filters.
Under those assumptions, averaging each phase gives a joint outcome law that depends only
on parity. The parity likelihood is therefore sufficient for the full outcome record, as
derived in v1.1; this exact reduction is not a validation of correlated residual phases.
The effective parity
contrast is C̄_par = C̄₁C̄₂/2 ≤ 1/2; contrasts above that ceiling require the full joint-readout
model and are labelled servo/coherent context.

The effective binary link is

\[
P(Y_n=1\mid\epsilon_n,\theta_n)
=\frac{1+\bar C\cos(\theta_n+\epsilon_n)}{2}.
\]

The hidden frequency process is stationary Gaussian OU noise, with covariance
σ_x² exp(−|Δ|/τ_c). Its accumulated free-Ramsey phase has variance

\[
s_\tau^2=2(g\sigma_x)^2
\left[\tau_c\tau-\tau_c^2(1-e^{-\tau/\tau_c})\right].
\]

For fixed, non-overlapping interrogation windows and cycle duration c = τ + t_dead,

\[
a_k=F(u)e^{-kc/\tau_c},\qquad
F(u)=\frac{\cosh u-1}{u-1+e^{-u}},\qquad u=\tau/\tau_c,\quad k\ge1.
\]

Here u is the interrogation-window ratio, not c/τ_c. Point sampling means u → 0. The
point-sampled phase is AR(1); the finite-window integrated phase generally requires the
ARMA(1,1) representation discussed under F. The closed forms are C07 (`result`), with the
model-consistency qualification in [note 12](../notes/2026-09-05-note-12-a2-noise-physics-provenance.md).

Three conventions remain separate:

| Calculation | Contrast and time convention |
|---|---|
| Same-τ comparator and policy campaigns B/C | C̄ or C_eff is effective per-shot contrast; correlation time is expressed in cycles, τ_c/c. Pilot: C_eff = 0.4, s = 0.5. Stress: C_eff = 0.5, s = 0.3, the parity ceiling and an upper-bound stress test. |
| Physical map C08 | Initial contrast C₀ acquires exponential baseline decay during interrogation; optimise the two readouts' rates per wall-clock time using c = τ + t_dead. |
| Identifiability E/C26 | Initial contrast C₀ with dephasing applied separately; τ/T₂ and τ_c/T₂ are dimensionless. Information is per shot and includes no dead-time cost. |

The [locked B/E manifest](../analysis/manifest_BE.json), its
[citation erratum in note 24](../notes/2026-09-14-note-24-tutorial-review.md), and the
[manifest checks](../tests/test_manifest_BE.py) preserve these distinctions.

## Committed paper hierarchy

### 1. Sequential-information crossover and physical regime map

The two Ramsey responses — contrast loss at a fringe extremum and pair correlation at
mid-fringe — are established noise-spectroscopy mechanisms. C02's exact correlator is prior
art, identified with Wudarski et al. (2023), Eq. 29, in the bounded A2 audit. The contribution
here is their sequential-information comparison and quantitative crossover, with attribution
as recorded in [A1](../notes/2026-09-05-note-11-prior-art-audit.md) and
[A2](../notes/2026-09-05-note-12-a2-noise-physics-provenance.md). Those searches do not establish
universal absence of prior art.

At the dark extremum, use the exact marginal Bernoulli divergence

\[
I_{\rm ext}=D_{\rm Bern}(p_1\Vert p_0),\qquad
p_0=(1-\bar C)/2,\quad p_1=(1-\bar C e^{-s^2/2})/2.
\]

This is the marginal-information comparator; it is not a claim that the full extremum
stream has no additional correlation information. C24 remains open. The small-s expansion
and its contrast-dependent accuracy are governed by C21 (`result`), replacing C01
(`withdrawn`).

At mid-fringe, C02 gives r_k = C̄² e^(−s²) sinh(s²a_k). The finite-amplitude reference is
the Gaussian spectral comparator built from those exact correlations. “Exact comparator”
names that calculation, not an exact binary likelihood rate. Its validated scope is C04
(`result`): agreement within Monte-Carlo uncertainty against the point-sampled latent-AR(1)
binary reference at the tested points. The slope-loss-resummed expression and strict O(s⁴)
asymptote are distinguished as in [note 14](../notes/2026-09-07-note-14-helper-split-and-BE-decisions.md).

The amplitude-free crossover is a small-s limiting theorem, where both leading-order
expansions apply:

\[
\sum_{k\ge1}a_k^2>\frac{1}{4\bar C^2(1-\bar C^2)}.
\]

For point sampling this becomes τ_c/c > 2/ln[1 + 4C̄²(1−C̄²)]. The finite-amplitude headline
keeps **C05's existing scope** (`result`): at C̄ = 0.4 the stated comparator crossover is
4.65 cycles as s → 0, 5.46 at s = 0.3 and 7.24 at s = 0.5. Its separate high-contrast
statement — no comparator crossover at C̄ = 0.99 for s ≥ 0.5 — remains servo/coherent context.

### 2. Detection versus identification under a calibrated baseline

This contribution has equal billing with item 1. An extremum alarm reports a dephasing change;
attribution to stochastic frequency noise depends on the admissible baseline and accessible
interrogation times. The τ-scan is a test of that distinction, not automatic identification.

For nonnegative shot weights w_τ summing to one, retain the two-nuisance definition

\[
\mathcal I_{\rm id}(w;\mathcal X)=
\inf_{\chi_s,\chi_0\in\mathcal X}
\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T}w_\tau
D_{\rm Bern}\!\left[p_{\rm sig}(\tau;\chi_s)\Vert p_0(\tau;\chi_0)\right],
\]

where p_sig = [1 − C₀ exp(−χ_s − s_τ²/2)]/2 and p₀ = [1 − C₀ exp(−χ₀)]/2. The signal-side
and null-side nuisance baselines vary independently within the same calibrated class.

Here Γ̂ = 1/T₂ is the calibrated baseline rate. **Class A** permits an independent
dephasing value at each scan point within
|χ(τ) − Γ̂τ| ≤ η₀Γ̂τ. Its marginal ambiguity disappears at a point only when
s_τ²/2 > 2η₀Γ̂τ. If the bands overlap at every weighted scan point, the class-A divergence
is zero. **Class B** requires χ(τ) = Γτ with |Γ − Γ̂| ≤ η₀Γ̂, so one rate must fit the entire
scan on each side. The numerical evidence concerns this Markov-parametric restriction;
it does not establish a result for every possible smoothness-constrained baseline.

**Change of admissible class:** v1.1's class was the pointwise band and explicitly did not
require a Markovian baseline.
[Note 03 §4](../notes/2026-09-02-note-03.md#4-τ-scan-identifiability-corollary-card-item-3)
applies “the two-nuisance divergence 𝓘_id of card v1.1” to both classes, and its
[Card implications item 1](../notes/2026-09-02-note-03.md#card-implications-mathematical-not-scope)
proposes changing the admissible class. That content establishes B as a proposed extension
to v1.1; Git history does not establish the writing order, since both files entered in the
same archive-import commit, `81058de`.
v2.0 compares the original type of band (A), specialised to the stated calibration, with
that added shape restriction (B). The one-sided variant that fixes χ_s = χ̂_φ and varies
only the null baseline is not carried into the v2.0 contents.

**C26 (`result`) supersedes C13 (`withdrawn`).** The completed E grid uses
C₀ ∈ {0.4, 0.5, 0.9}, η₀ ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, τ_c/T₂ ∈ {0.05, 0.2, 1, 5},
gσ_xT₂ = 0.5 and 40 interrogation times over τ/T₂ ∈ [0.1, 2]. C₀ = 0.4 and 0.5 are the
principal slices; C₀ = 0.9 is servo/coherent context. Information and retained fractions
refer to equal shot allocation over that scan, without dead time.

The complete class-A zero set contains **18 of 36 rows**: all nine at τ_c/T₂ = 0.05,
six at τ_c/T₂ = 0.2 with η₀ ≥ 0.05, and three at τ_c/T₂ = 1 with η₀ = 0.10.
Each group includes C₀ ∈ {0.4, 0.5, 0.9}. The last group supplements the correct but
incomplete list in C26 and note 15; it is recorded in
[note 25](../notes/2026-09-15-note-25-freeze-review-evidence.md) without editing either.
At τ_c/T₂ = 0.2 and η₀ = 0.02 the band retains about 3.6 × 10⁻³ of the
known-baseline information, with 21 of 40 scan points passing the separation guard. This
boundary case is difficult identification, not exact non-identifiability.

On all 18 zero rows, v1.1's go/no-go condition 4 is not met under the stated pointwise
class and finite scan. Positive retained-information estimates on those rows belong to
the added class B. This is the reinterpretation of the
extremum branch called for by v1.1's stop rule, not a demonstration that its original
identification condition held. It does not erase the nonzero class-A boundary case above.

Write J_B for the true class-B infimum and I_B for the reported best feasible candidate.
Because B is contained in A, and every reported candidate is feasible,

\[
I_A\;\le\;J_B\;\le\;I_B.
\]

The reported I_B is obtained from three named optimiser starts plus a deterministic
121 × 121 grid scan and polish. Grid stability does not certify a global infimum. A missed
lower candidate would reduce the retained-information estimate; a positive candidate alone
does not prove J_B > 0. The corresponding class-B retained fractions are candidate estimates,
with the bound direction preserved when divided by the same positive known-baseline rate.

In the fast-noise limit, ΔΓ_φ = (gσ_x)²τ_c and the band-separation criterion is
ΔΓ_φ/Γ̂ > 2η₀. On the committed grid, ΔΓ_φ/Γ̂ = 0.25 τ_c/T₂. For class-A separation on
the finite interrogation-time scan, this fast-noise inequality is **necessary but not
sufficient**: the finite-window exponent lies below its fast-noise limit, and the actual
s_τ²/2 > 2η₀Γ̂τ guard must be checked. It is not a universal identification threshold for
the more restrictive class B.

The τ_c/T₂ = 1, η₀ = 0.10 rows illustrate the limit directly: the fast-noise ratio
ΔΓ_φ/(2η₀Γ̂) is 1.25, yet none of the 40 scan points separates and I_A = 0. The class-B
candidates retain about 8.7–10.4 % of the known-baseline information across the three
contrasts, with the same upper-bound qualification as above.

Evidence: [E closure and C26](../notes/2026-09-07-note-16-E-closure.md),
[res9](../analysis/outputs/res9_identifiability_T2.json), and
[note 24's scope and bound corrections](../notes/2026-09-14-note-24-tutorial-review.md).
