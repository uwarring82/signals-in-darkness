## Research question

How should synchronously interrogated quantum clocks choose their Ramsey operating point and interrogation timing to detect weak stochastic frequency modulation as quickly as possible?

The paper addresses:

1. When does fringe-extremum operation, which detects contrast loss, outperform mid-fringe operation, which detects temporal correlations?
2. When can the extremum signal be distinguished from an ordinary change in T₂?
3. What wall-clock delay is incurred when the signal correlation time is unknown and must be learned while measurements are selected?

The central contribution is the interaction between identifiability, model learning, and measurement selection.

## Motivation

A zero-mean stochastic phase modulation appears differently at different positions on a Ramsey fringe.

- At an extremum, it changes the marginal bit probability through contrast loss.
- At mid-fringe, it leaves the mean unchanged but creates temporal correlations.

The preferred measurement therefore depends on the hidden process's correlation time. Moreover, the extremum response can be indistinguishable from ordinary dephasing unless the experiment varies its interrogation duration.

**[v1.1]** At finite amplitude and long correlation time the mid-fringe channel changes character: from fourth-order variance detection to first-order tracking of a quasi-static offset. This is the physical reason the small-amplitude expansion fails in the region where mid-fringe operation is preferred.

This problem arises in differential clock comparisons, stochastic-field sensing, and qubit noise spectroscopy. The paper studies the general detection boundary rather than a particular clock platform or application.

## Paired-clock observation model

Two clocks or qubits are synchronously interrogated by a common local oscillator. Their outcomes are

\[
B_{1,n},B_{2,n}\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

Conditional on oscillator phase \(\varphi_n\), differential signal phase \(\epsilon_n\), and action \(u_n\),

\[
P(B_{1,n},B_{2,n}\mid\varphi_n,\epsilon_n,u_n)
=
\frac14
\left[
1+B_{1,n}\bar C_1(\tau_n)
\cos(\varphi_n+\phi_{1,n})
\right]
\left[
1+B_{2,n}\bar C_2(\tau_n)
\cos(\varphi_n+\epsilon_n+\phi_{2,n})
\right].
\]

The effective contrasts include decoherence and readout loss:

\[
\bar C_j(\tau)
=
\bar C_{0,j}
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{\tau}{2T_{1,j}}-\chi_{\phi,j}(\tau)
\right],
\]

reducing to \(\bar C_{0,j}e^{-\tau/T_{2,j}}\) under exponential Markovian decoherence.

The Ramsey filter function is denoted \(m_n(t)\). Common-mode oscillator rejection requires

\[
\tau_{1,n}=\tau_{2,n},\qquad m_{1,n}(t)=m_{2,n}(t).
\]

If the filters differ, residual oscillator noise remains explicitly in the likelihood.

## Two oscillator regimes

### Fully randomized oscillator: parity readout

Marginalizing a uniformly random oscillator phase gives

\[
P(B_{1,n},B_{2,n}\mid\epsilon_n)
=
\frac14
\left[
1+\frac12B_{1,n}B_{2,n}\bar C_1\bar C_2\cos(\epsilon_n+\Delta\phi_n)
\right].
\]

The informative statistic is the parity \(Z_n=B_{1,n}B_{2,n}\), with effective contrast

\[
\boxed{\bar C_{\mathrm{par}}=\tfrac12\bar C_1\bar C_2\leq\tfrac12}.
\]

Consequently \(p_0=(1-\bar C_{\mathrm{par}})/2\geq1/4\). Parity correlation spectroscopy never produces a genuinely dark fringe. For realistic individual contrasts \(\bar C_j\simeq0.9\), \(\bar C_{\mathrm{par}}\simeq0.405\).

### Partially coherent oscillator: full joint readout

For a partially coherent oscillator, use the von Mises phase model

\[
q_\kappa(\varphi)=\frac{e^{\kappa\cos(\varphi-\mu)}}{2\pi I_0(\kappa)}.
\]

The mean phase \(\mu\) must be tracked by a servo. Servo interrogations and their dead time are part of the wall-clock cost for both the oracle and the adaptive learner.

The physical likelihood is the von Mises average of the four-outcome law. When \(\kappa>0\), the individual bits carry information in addition to parity. Effective contrasts above 1/2, including near-dark fringes, belong to this regime and require the full four-outcome likelihood with residual oscillator noise.

The principal figure distinguishes \(\bar C\leq1/2\) (parity-only, oscillator-cancelled) from \(\bar C>1/2\) (servo-tracked, partially coherent).

## Hidden process

The first paper uses stationary Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck frequency noise,

\[
\mathbb E[x(t)x(t+\Delta)]=\sigma_x^2e^{-|\Delta|/\tau_c},
\]

with accumulated Ramsey phase \(\epsilon_n=g\int_{t_n}^{t_n+\tau}m_n(t)x(t)\,dt\). For free Ramsey evolution,

\[
s_\tau^2=2g^2\sigma_x^2\left[\tau_c\tau-\tau_c^2\left(1-e^{-\tau/\tau_c}\right)\right],
\qquad
s_\tau^2\sim
\begin{cases}
g^2\sigma_x^2\tau^2, & \tau\ll\tau_c,\\
2g^2\sigma_x^2\tau_c\tau, & \tau\gg\tau_c.
\end{cases}
\]

**[v1.1]** The normalized shot correlation has the closed form (verified against numerical integration)

\[
a_k=e^{-kc/\tau_c}\,F(u),\qquad
F(u)=\frac{\cosh u-1}{u-1+e^{-u}},\qquad u=\tau/\tau_c,
\]

with \(F(u)\to1\) in the point-sampling limit and \(a_1\simeq(\tau_c/2\tau)\,e^{-(c-\tau)/\tau_c}\) for \(\tau\gg\tau_c\): long interrogations wash out shot-to-shot correlation.

**[v1.1]** At mid-fringe the exact lag correlation of the bits is

\[
\boxed{r_k=\bar C^2\,e^{-s_\tau^2}\sinh\!\left(s_\tau^2a_k\right)}
\;\simeq\;\bar C^2s_\tau^2a_k\,e^{-s_\tau^2}.
\]

The factor \(e^{-s_\tau^2}\) is slope loss from the same modulation and is the mid-fringe counterpart of extremum contrast loss.

The known signal quantity is \(g\sigma_x\). The learner does not know \(\tau_c\); \(r_k\) carries \(\tau_c\) through both amplitude and lag dependence, so two or more lags make it learnable in principle.

## Null and the meaning of the extremum branch

The null includes a calibrated differential-decoherence baseline,

\[
H_0:\quad\epsilon_n=0,\qquad\bar C_j(\tau,n)=\bar C_{j,0}(\tau)+\delta C_{j,n},
\]

with \(\delta C_{j,n}\) covering differential \(T_2\) variation, laser-power drift, readout drift, and unexplained dephasing.

At the extremum the OU process produces \(\bar C_{\mathrm{obs}}(\tau)=\bar C_0(\tau)e^{-s_\tau^2/2}\). For \(\tau\gg\tau_c\) this is \(e^{-g^2\sigma_x^2\tau_c\tau}\), exactly exponential in \(\tau\) and indistinguishable from an additional Markovian dephasing rate \(\Delta\Gamma_\phi=g^2\sigma_x^2\tau_c\). In the short-correlation regime where the crossover favors the extremum, the extremum is a \(T_2\) measurement. For \(\tau\ll\tau_c\) the signature \(e^{-g^2\sigma_x^2\tau^2/2}\) is Gaussian in \(\tau\).

Specific attribution after an extremum-triggered detection therefore requires multi-\(\tau\) validation against the calibrated dephasing baseline; the action set performs identifiability work. A mid-fringe detection may already be specific through its temporal correlations and does not require the scan. Echo interleaving suppresses slow noise most effectively when \(\tau_c\gg\tau\), precisely where the correlation channel is already favored.

**[v1.1]** *Admissible baseline class.* Let \(\hat\chi_\phi(\tau)\) be the calibrated dephasing function and \(\eta(\tau)\) its calibration uncertainty. The admissible class is

\[
\mathcal X_0=\{\chi:\ |\chi(\tau)-\hat\chi_\phi(\tau)|\le\eta(\tau)\ \ \forall\tau\in\mathcal T\},
\]

over the scanned set \(\mathcal T\). With independent nuisance curves on the two sides, define the identifiability divergence for a shot allocation \(w_\tau\),

\[
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{id}}(w)=
\inf_{\chi_s,\chi_0\in\mathcal X_0}
\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T}w_\tau\,
D_{\mathrm{Bern}}\!\left[p_{\mathrm{sig}}(\tau;\chi_s)\,\Vert\,p_0(\tau;\chi_0)\right],
\]

\[
p_{\mathrm{sig}}(\tau;\chi_s)=\frac{1-\bar C_0(\tau)e^{-\chi_s(\tau)-s_\tau^2/2}}{2},
\qquad
p_0(\tau;\chi_0)=\frac{1-\bar C_0(\tau)e^{-\chi_0(\tau)}}{2}.
\]

The extremum signal is *distinguishable* on \(\mathcal T\) under allocation \(w\) if \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm{id}}(w)\) exceeds the detection threshold. The criterion is zero whenever an admissible baseline-only curve can absorb \(s_\tau^2/2\) across the scan, which is the mimicry it must test. A less conservative variant fixes \(\chi_s=\hat\chi_\phi\) and minimises only over the competing null curve \(\chi_0\). The baseline need not be Markovian; the class \(\mathcal X_0\) replaces the word "nonexponential".

> The extremum branch detects a change in dephasing. It identifies stochastic frequency noise only when the accessible \(\tau\)-dependence is demonstrably distinct from the calibrated baseline.

## Principal operating-point theorem

Consider the calibrated effective binary link \(P(Y_n=1\mid\epsilon_n,\theta)=\tfrac12[1+\bar C\cos(\theta+\epsilon_n)]\).

### Extremum information (unchanged)

At \(\theta=\pi\): \(p_0=(1-\bar C)/2\), \(p_1=(1-\bar Ce^{-s^2/2})/2\), and

\[
I_{\mathrm{ext}}=D_{\mathrm{Bern}}(p_1\Vert p_0)
=\frac{\bar C^2s^4}{8(1-\bar C^2)}+o(s^4),
\qquad\text{valid for }s^2\ll\frac{1-\bar C^2}{\bar C}.
\]

Numerically, the expansion is within 6 % for \(s^2\bar C/(1-\bar C^2)\le0.06\) and within 20 % up to 0.17. The exact divergence is used wherever the condition is not met. **[v1.1]** At high contrast the extremum bit stream may carry an additional ~10 % of correlation information beyond \(D_{\mathrm{Bern}}\); this is an open numerical item and is not incorporated.

### Mid-fringe information **[v1.1, replaces v1.0]**

At \(\theta=\pi/2\) the marginal probability remains 1/2; information sits in the lag correlations \(r_k\) above.

*Leading order (s → 0 corollary only).* \(I_{\mathrm{mid}}=\tfrac12\sum_{k\ge1}r_k^2+o(s^4)=\tfrac12\bar C^4s^4e^{-2s^2}\sum_k a_k^2+\dots\), valid only under the spectral condition

\[
\boxed{\sup_\omega\Big|2\sum_{k\ge1}r_k\cos k\omega\Big|\ll1
\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;
\delta(0)=2\sum_{k\ge1}r_k\approx2\bar C^2s^2\frac{\tau_c}{c}\ll1
\quad\text{(positive OU correlations)}}.
\]

The v1.0 condition \(\bar C^4s^4\Sigma a_k^2\ll1\) is withdrawn: it is satisfied (0.015) at points where the expansion is already off by 2.5×.

*Working finite-amplitude rate.* Define the Gaussian spectral comparator

\[
I^{\mathrm{GP}}_{\mathrm{mid}}=\frac12\int_0^{1}\big[S(f)-1-\log S(f)\big]\,df,
\qquad
S(f)-1=2\sum_{k\ge1}r_k\cos(2\pi fk).
\]

With \(r_k=\bar C^2e^{-s^2}\sinh(s^2Aa^k)\) the sinh Taylor expansion represents the spectrum exactly as an infinite series of geometric spectra,

\[
S(\omega)-1=\sum_{m\ge0}2\rho_m\frac{a_m\cos\omega-a_m^2}{1-2a_m\cos\omega+a_m^2},
\qquad
\rho_m=\bar C^2e^{-s^2}\frac{(s^2A)^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!},\quad a_m=a^{2m+1}.
\]

Six terms are a controlled numerical truncation for \(s^2A\le1\). The \(m=0\) term alone has the closed form \(-\tfrac12\log[\alpha/(1+b^2)]\), \(\alpha=1+a^2-2\rho_0a^2\), \(\beta=2a(\rho_0-1)\), \(b=(-\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^2-\beta^2})/\beta\), adequate to 2 % for \(s\le0.5\) and to 0.4 % at the mid-fringe-optimal \(\tau\) across the pilot map; it fails at \(s=1\).

*Validation.* The comparator agrees with the grid-converged latent-AR(1) binary reference within Monte-Carlo uncertainty (standard error ≈ 7 % at 10⁵ shots) in all seven cases tested, spanning \(\bar C\in\{0.4,0.9\}\), \(s\in[0.2,1.0]\), \(\tau_c/c\in[1,60]\), and including cases with \(\delta(0)\) up to 3.7 where the leading-order formula is off by 4×. It is called a validated working comparator, not the exact binary rate.

*Tracking limit.* For \(\delta(0)\gg1\) the comparator rate rises only logarithmically and approaches the quasi-static tracking scale \(\sim\bar C^2s^2/2\): first order in \(s^2\), no longer fourth.

### Crossover **[v1.1]**

*Theorem (s → 0 limit).* Where both leading-order expansions are valid, mid-fringe wins iff

\[
\boxed{\sum_{k\ge1}a_k^2>\frac{1}{4\bar C^2(1-\bar C^2)}},
\]

which for point-sampled OU is \(\tau_c/c>2/\ln[1+4\bar C^2(1-\bar C^2)]\): U-shaped in \(\bar C\), minimum 2.9 shots at \(\bar C=1/\sqrt2\), 4.65 at \(\bar C=0.4\), 3.6 at 0.5, 4.2 at 0.9, 26.6 at 0.99.

*Finite amplitude (comparator vs exact extremum divergence, point-sampled).* Crossover \(\tau_c/c\):

| \(\bar C\) | s → 0 | s = 0.3 | s = 0.5 | s = 1.0 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.40 | 4.65 | 5.46 | 7.24 | 40 |
| 0.50 | 3.57 | 4.20 | 5.46 | ≈ 30 |
| 0.70 | 2.89 | 3.36 | 4.47 | 29 |
| 0.90 | 4.17 | 5.13 | 7.62 | 320 |
| 0.99 | 26.6 | 72 | never | never |

The theorem is exact as a limit; the finite-amplitude crossover depends on \(s\) and disappears at very high contrast. The reference detector confirms the s = 0.5, \(\bar C\)=0.4 crossover near 6–7.

## Endpoint-optimality lemma

With \(x=\cos^2\theta\) the leading information is \(I(x)=A\,x/(1-\bar C^2x)+B(1-x)^2/(1-\bar C^2x)^2\), \(A=\bar C^2s^4/8\), \(B=\tfrac12\bar C^4s^4e^{-2s^2}\Sigma a_k^2\). Mean and correlation perturbations are orthogonal in the null \(\chi^2\) metric (one-body vs two-body centered statistics), so their contributions add at leading order. The derivative numerator \(A(1-\bar C^2x)-2B(1-\bar C^2)(1-x)\) is linear in \(x\) and positive at \(x=1\); it is either positive throughout or crosses once from negative to positive, giving an interior minimum. The optimum is an endpoint under balanced readout.

**[v1.1]** Reference-detector profiles are consistent with the interior minimum and with no interior point beating the endpoints; near the crossover they are consistent with flatness within approximately 5 % over \(\theta\in[60°,90°]\) given the stated Monte-Carlo uncertainty, so intermediate phases near mid-fringe cost little. Asymmetric readout may move the optimum inward and is checked with the exact likelihood.

## Adaptive policy

The clean information about \(\tau_c\) is contained in mid-fringe or intermediate-phase correlations at fixed \(\tau\); a \(\tau\)-scan at the extremum is the alternative exploration route via the knee of \(s_\tau^2\) at \(\tau\approx\tau_c\), available when \(\tau_c<T_2\) and the baseline \(\chi_\phi(\tau)\) is calibrated. Both routes are admitted; which is cheaper is regime-dependent and is itself a result.

Policy structure: maintain the common-oscillator servo; begin at mid-fringe and measure at least two lags (or begin a \(\tau\)-scan); update the posterior over \(\tau_c\); remain at mid-fringe when the correlated regime is supported; migrate toward the extremum only when short correlation is supported; after any extremum-triggered detection, interleave multiple \(\tau\) and test distinguishability against \(\mathcal X_0\) before attributing contrast loss to stochastic frequency noise. If the \(\tau\)-signature cannot distinguish the two, the output is "additional dephasing detected". A mid-fringe-triggered detection carries its own temporal-structure evidence and is reported as such.

The oracle knows \(\tau_c\) and \(g\sigma_x\) but not the realized trajectory or change time; the learner knows \(g\sigma_x\) and the OU family. Both pay the same servo and calibration costs. **[v1.1]** The grid-HMM bank over candidate \(\tau_c\) makes posterior updating tractable (predictive likelihoods per candidate); the spectral comparator is not an observation likelihood and does not update the posterior — it makes scoring of candidate actions by expected information rate cheap.

**[v1.1]** The task is fixed as generic change detection for the regret panel. Specific identification is reported separately. After an extremum-triggered alarm, both oracle and learner must pay for multi-\(\tau\) validation; a mid-fringe alarm may instead establish specificity through its temporal-correlation evidence.

**[v1.1]** Regret is defined at matched *measured* null run length:

\[
R_{\mathrm{learn}}(\tau_c;\gamma)=\mathbb E_{\tau_c}[T_{\mathrm{adaptive}}-\nu]-\mathbb E_{\tau_c}[T_{\mathrm{oracle}}-\nu],
\qquad
\hat{\mathbb E}_0[T_{\mathrm{adaptive}}]=\hat{\mathbb E}_0[T_{\mathrm{oracle}}]=\gamma.
\]

## False-alarm control **[v1.1, replaces v1.0]**

Every action has its own null law \(P_0(Y_n\mid u_n)\). For each fixed candidate change time, the action-conditional likelihood-ratio process is an \(H_0\)-martingale under predictable actions. The reflected, filter-reset CUSUM used numerically is not itself that martingale; its policy-dependent null run length is therefore calibrated by simulation — bisection on \(h\) until the measured \(\hat{\mathbb E}_0[T]\) reaches the target, with standard errors reported — separately for each operating point and for the complete adaptive policy including its switches. No universal \(e^h\) run-length guarantee is invoked for the dependent latent-HMM model without a separate proof.

Remark (not relied on). For the specific reset rule used here — restart with the filter at its stationary prior whenever the segment likelihood ratio falls below 1 — the detector is a sequence of SPRTs with boundaries \((1,e^h)\); Ville's inequality applied to each segment's martingale gives success probability \(\le e^{-h}\) per segment and hence \(\mathbb E_0[T]\ge e^h\) under the conditionally independent, action-conditional null used in the pilot. This two-line argument does not extend to max-over-change-time or filter-bank CUSUM forms, and in any case the measured slack is 10²–10⁴ (extremum: ARL 30 300 at \(h=3.70\), \(e^h=40\); mid-fringe: slack 590–7 200 depending on \(\tau_c\)), so it is never used for calibration.

## Pilot finite-γ observation **[v1.1]**

At \(\bar C=0.4\), \(s=0.5\), matched \(\hat{\mathbb E}_0[T]\approx3\times10^4\) (extremum 30 300 ± 1 900; mid-fringe 37 400 ± 5 600, 26 100 ± 3 000, 39 100 ± 4 500 at \(\tau_c/c=20,5,1\)):

| \(\tau_c/c\) | extremum delay | mid-fringe delay | delay ratio ext/mid | rate ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 2 140 ± 110 | 1 370 ± 140 | 1.56 | 2.36 |
| 5 | 2 170 ± 100 | 2 660 ± 280 | 0.82 | 0.69 |
| 1 | 2 160 ± 110 | 12 100 ± 1 400 | 0.18 | 0.06 |

The delay crossover lies between \(\tau_c/c=5\) and 20, consistent with the comparator crossover at 7.2. The finite-threshold delay advantage is smaller than the rate ratio by ≈ 1.5–3. Because the matched run lengths agree only within their uncertainties, this is a pilot observation; tighter null statistics are required before the compression factor is quoted as a result.

## Go/no-go conditions **[v1.1 additions marked]**

Proceed only if:

1. the full paired-clock likelihood preserves the crossover;
2. the crossover intersects physically accessible parity or servo regimes — *pilot: the mathematical same-\(\tau\) map crosses at roughly 5–7 cycles for \(\bar C\simeq0.4\), \(s\le0.5\); after optimising interrogation time the physical boundary moves to several \(T_2\) for the stated \(T_2/t_{\rm dead}\) and \(g\sigma_xT_2\)*;
3. the extremum advantage survives the contrast-nuisance model;
4. the accessible \(\tau\)-signature distinguishes the extremum signal from a dephasing-rate change relative to the calibrated baseline;
5. the adaptive learner approaches the achievable oracle with interpretable wall-clock regret at matched measured \(\hat{\mathbb E}_0[T]\);
6. useful actions remain compatible with common-mode rejection and servo costs;
7. the result is not a direct substitution into an existing active-testing theorem;
8. **[v1.1]** the comparator remains within Monte-Carlo agreement with the reference detector across the plotted domain of panel (ii);
9. **[v1.1]** the finite-γ compression is reproduced with tightened null statistics.

Stop or reinterpret the extremum branch if it can establish only that \(T_2\) changed. Stop the paper if the remaining result reduces to ordinary sequential discrimination of known Bernoulli channels. *Withdrawn as a kill criterion:* "nonperturbative binary corrections remove the crossover" — tested; they shift it (≈ 50 % at \(s=0.5\)) and remove it only at \(\bar C\ge0.99\), which is now part of the result.
